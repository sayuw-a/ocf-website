# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, treated as equally important: students new to OCF (Orthodox students who haven't yet connected, inquirers from other backgrounds, and anyone with a sincere curiosity about the Faith) and current OCF members looking up event times, resources, or contact information. New visitors arrive not knowing what to expect and need to quickly understand what OCF actually is and what showing up looks like; members return for logistics they already trust the group to provide.

## Product Purpose

The site exists to get students to show up in person — to a gathering, a service, or an event — not merely to inform them about Orthodoxy in the abstract. Every page should reduce the distance between "I found this site" and "I walked into a room." Success is measured in attendance, not just page views or comprehension.

## Positioning

OCF at UVA is student participation in the actual sacramental and liturgical life of the Orthodox Church — worship and Divine Liturgy at a real parish, not a campus discussion group or affinity club with a religious theme. The site should read as an extension of the Church's life on Grounds, not as a student-org page that happens to be about religion.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: Primary — "Start Here if You're New" (drive toward attending). Secondary/fallback — "Email a student leader" for anyone not ready to show up yet.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: This is a real, welcoming door into the Orthodox Church on campus — you don't need to know anything or be a member to walk through it.
- Belief ladder: (1) This is a legitimate, ongoing presence of the Orthodox Church at UVA, not an informal club. (2) It's genuinely welcoming to people at any stage — cradle Orthodox, inquirers, and the merely curious alike. (3) Showing up is low-stakes: no prior knowledge or commitment required. (4) I know concretely what to expect and where/when to go.
- Proof on hand: none collected yet. Group photos already in use on the homepage (`/images/group-2023.jpg` and similar) serve as informal social proof; no testimonials, press, or partner logos on file.

## Brand Personality

Warm, reverent, unhurried. The tone is contemplative rather than energetic — grounded in the rhythm of the Church calendar rather than campus-club enthusiasm. Confidence comes from quiet consistency, not salesmanship.

## Anti-references

Two failure modes to avoid explicitly: (1) the generic SaaS/startup landing page — gradient hero banners, bold sans-only headlines, feature-grid cards, anything that reads as a pitch deck; (2) the corporate megachurch site — stock photography of worship bands, stage lighting, and salesy CTA language. The current page on screen is a reasonable starting identity (warm cream background, amber accent, serif display paired with humanist sans) and is not itself an anti-reference — departures from it need a specific reason, not a general "make it more exciting."

## Design Principles

- Reverence over hype: let content and photography carry weight instead of decorative flourishes or urgency-driven copy.
- Low-stakes welcome: every page should make the smallest possible next step (show up once, ask a question, email someone) the most obvious action.
- Church rhythm, not club calendar: information architecture and language should reflect the liturgical/weekly rhythm of the Church, not generic "events" framing.
- One tone across both audiences: newcomer-facing warmth and member-facing utility should feel like the same voice, not two different sites stitched together.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA baseline: sufficient color contrast, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, meaningful alt text on photography, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion`. No additional known accommodation needs beyond this baseline.
